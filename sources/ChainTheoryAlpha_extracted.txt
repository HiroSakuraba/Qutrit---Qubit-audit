                                                          S1.    THE MODEL

   The axiom reads: Nothing exists but finitely many maximally entangled pairs of qudits, some qudits shared, known
only by distilling them. Its five definitions (qudit, shared, distilling, read, known) spell out its terms, and the properties
of each that the Letter uses follow from it as the corollaries below. Lemma S1 supplies the matchings used in the first.
Corollary S1 (A qudit). The state space of an end is the span of the outputs of the acts on it, the pair kets under
the admissible matchings. At the qutrit–qubit link that span is the whole six-dimensional tensor space. The moduli of
its inner products are fixed by the Born readout; their phases beyond the moduli are fixed by no readout.
Proof. The acts on an end are distillations, whose outputs are pair kets on the common support, carried by the
matchings over the orbit of the pair ket. At (2, 3) the orbit under the fourth-root matchings at both ends has 96
vectors and spans all 6 dimensions, as does the cyclic module generated from the pair ket by the matrix units of the
two ends; the same holds at every type with ends of dimension two to four. The frame operator of the orbit is a
scalar, so the Born readouts, the squared moduli of the Gram elements at all 576 pairs of the frame, fix the moduli
of the inner products. The remaining phases are moved by matchings that fix every readout.
  A pair of the axiom of type (d1 , d2 ), also called a link, joins two qudits of dimensions d1 , d2 in the pair ket on their
common support, and distilling two qudits outputs the pair of their type. A qudit is shared when it is one end of two
or more pairs, spelling out “some qudits shared”, and its copies, one per pair, are one qudit, so an exchange of its
pairs leaves it in place and moves only their far ends.

Corollary S2 (Distilling). An act on two qudits whose output is a pair of the two on their common support is exactly
the projection Pm onto a maximally entangled pair there.

Proof. Nothing exists but maximally entangled pairs, so an act on two qudits leaves them in a pair or in nothing. A
unitary preserves the purity of its input and makes no pair; a local measurement, or a label of one, leaves no pure
state; a replacement channel leaves the partners in no pair; a sub-maximal projection reaches no configuration of the
axiom. The rank of every operator equals the Schmidt rank of its output across the cut, and Pm returns the pair, so
the act is Pm and nothing else.

Corollary S3 (A read). (i) A read, a distillation of the two ends of a pair in a basis of one end, one read per basis,
has at most d+1 bases at an end of dimension d: the mutually unbiased eigenbases of the clock and shift operators [31],
three at d = 2 and four at d = 3. (ii) The basis a read gives its qudit is the label it attaches beyond the projection and
passes along the pair. (iii) At each element of its basis, the read determines the state of the far end with no freedom.
(iv) The basis a read passes reaches the far end whole, so a read is taken at the larger end of a pair, or at either end
of an equal one.

Proof. For (iii), ⟨ei |ω⟩ ∝ |ei ⟩ transported to the far end, by the transpose identity (A ⊗ 11)|ω⟩ = (11 ⊗ AT )|ω⟩ at every
matrix unit of the support. At the qutrit–qubit link the far direction is determined at all four bases of the qutrit,
152 of 152 matrix elements, with no sign left free. These far-end states need not form an orthonormal basis of the far
end: a qutrit basis gives three qubit states, one of them zero when a basis vector lies outside the common support.
For (iv), the far-end states lie in the common support and span it, which is the whole far end exactly when the far
end is no larger than the end at which the read is taken.

Corollary S4 (The known and the commutator records). What is known of a qudit is what its reads fix jointly, under
every matching of its pairs and every exchange of its reads or of its pairs. To fix is to determine with certainty on the
one copy (Corollary S5), and certainty leaves nothing open: no expectation value is estimated anywhere, and a read
fixes two ends only when every consistent relation between them is of a kind distillations carry (Sec. S2). Two reads
that anticommute therefore fix a further record, their product, with their order as its sign.

Proof. The first clause restates the axiom (“known only by distilling”) with the definition of a read. For the second,
two reads A and B compose to AB = 12 {A, B} + 21 [A, B], the first part fixed whatever their order and the second by
it. When they anticommute the first part vanishes, and the composite is fixed by the two reads with their order as
its sign. The letters of two of the bases of Corollary S3(i) are the clock and shift operators, whose commutator phase
is a primitive d-th root of unity. At the qubit end it is −1 for all three pairs of bases, so two reads there compose in
their two orders to one further operator with the order as its sign. That operator is traceless, outside the span of the
identity and the two reads, and fixed under every matching, so it is a record by the first clause. Only a sign can play
this role. For two invertible reads with AB = λBA at an end of dimension d, determinants give λd = 1, and reversing
the order multiplies the composite by λ−1 one way and by λ the other, so the order labels it, as an eigenvalue of
exchanging the two reads, an involution, only if λ = λ−1 . Together λgcd(d,2) = 1: a sign is possible only at even d,
which the qubit’s reads realize, and at the qutrit no two reads anticommute, their Weyl phase being a cube root of
unity, which is not its own inverse.

Corollary S5 (A single copy). No link between the same two qudits exists twice, and no act makes one, so a read
acts once on the one copy of the link it reads.

Proof. The pairs are finitely many states of the named qudits, and distilling, the one act, projects existing qudits and
creates none. A second link between the same two qudits in the same state would be a further entry of the pair list
at the same named qudits, which no act produces. Statistics over fresh instances of one link, and a repeated read
of one link on a fresh copy, are therefore unavailable. Distinct links in the same state at distinct qudits, such as the
three links of the object, are not excluded.

(the copies of Sec. S1). What a single read can fail to fix is therefore not a state but a relation between the two ends
of a type, the way the reads relate their operator algebras.

Lemma S4 (What reads carry). A linear map Λ : Md1 → Md2 is decomposable if Λ = Φ1 + Φ2 ◦ T with Φ1 , Φ2
completely positive and T the transpose. Every map between the two ends of a link that reads compose, by composition
and by taking reads jointly, is decomposable.

Proof. A read acts at its own end by the projection onto its pair and by the matchings U ⊗ Ū , which are completely
positive, and carries an operator to the far end by the transpose identity (A ⊗ 11)|ω⟩ = (11 ⊗ AT )|ω⟩, which is T on
the support. For Φ completely positive with Kraus operators K, T ◦ Φ ◦ T has Kraus operators K̄ and is completely
positive, and T ◦ T is the identity. So every composite is completely positive or completely positive after T , and reads
taken jointly add such maps.

   Certainty at the ends of a pair. A positive map between the operator algebras of two ends is a consistent way
of relating them: it carries every description of one end, a positive operator, to a description of the other, and the
matchings are its unitary cases. Reads carry only maps of the decomposable kinds (Lemma S4). A type is readable
when every positive map between its ends is decomposable and dark otherwise. To fix is to determine with certainty,
and certainty leaves nothing open: a read fixes two ends only when every consistent relation between them is of a kind
distillations carry, since a relation of another kind would leave open how one end describes the other. At a qudit
the relations the pair state leaves free are the matchings, all of a kind reads carry, and the known is what the reads
fix under every one of them and under every exchange of reads or of pairs (Corollary S4). At the ends of a pair the
clause ranges over every consistent relation between them, not only over the matchings, and a relation of a kind no
distillation carries is one the ends admit and no distillation could fix. The positive maps between two ends depend
only on their dimensions, and so does readability, so a single read fixes the ends of a pair exactly when its type is
readable. Under a weaker definition of fix, blind to the relations between ends, the read cap below would be a premise
rather than a consequence.
   Where the cap sits. Every positive map is decomposable at (2, 2) [35] and at (2, 3) and (3, 2) [36]. Non-
decomposable positive maps exist at (2, 4) [36] and at (3, 3) [37], Choi’s map, and compressing to a subspace and
embedding carry them to every type with d1 d2 ≥ 8. No product of two integers ≥ 2 equals seven. So a type of
dimensions d1 , d2 ≥ 2 is readable, and a single read fixes its ends, if and only if d1 d2 ≤ 6: the read cap, the form
in which the Letter and the Lean file use it. A dark type’s links stay maximally entangled and distillable; what no
distillation carries is a relation between their ends, not a state.

entanglement for every state of the type [20]. At d1 d2 ≥ 8 the maps that reads cannot carry detect entangled states

   The ancillary file ChainAlpha.lean is a single Lean 4 file on the core library alone, with no Mathlib and no imports;
lean ChainAlpha.lean checks it with Lean 4.35.0 in about a hundred seconds. Every computation is checked by the
kernel. The file contains no sorry, no axiom declaration, and no native code. Its main theorem, ChainAlpha.main,
has no hypotheses beyond the definitions of the file’s Part 1. It states that the object is a two-point configuration of
the qutrit–qubit link, that reads at the qutrit fix the qubit while reads at the qubit do not fix the qutrit, that every
record, built from the slots and the √ commutator
                                              √      matrices,
                                                     √         is an eigenvector of the exchange of the reads, and that
the normalized readout is (79 + 48 2 + 42 3 + 3 6)/31104. It depends  √       on propositional extensionality alone. The
file computes the two traces that define the readout, the sum of kr over the records and the readout dimension,
without constructing the readout space itself.
   The definitions of Part 1 are those of Table S1: the axiom as a structure of qudits and links; its five definitions, with
the definition of fix and the three clauses of Table S1, from which the file builds the slots and their letters, the records
with their own signs and the commutator records, the two exchanges and survival, and the weight function; and the
two definitions of the computed quantity, the two-point configuration and the normalized readout. Outside comments,
the digits 79, 48, 42, and 31104 and the counts 169 and 172 occur only in theorem statements, so they are outputs
and not inputs. Table S6 maps the results of this paper to the declarations. The ancillary script mutations.py
perturbs the definitions one at a time; every perturbation that changes a value at the object makes the main theorem
or commutator_records fail.

the_cap shows that readability by reads, with the qubit and the qutrit as readers and the far end absorbing the
